We report complete results of the downstream classifiers for all of the experiments in this project. We select five aspects, each varying by one factor: (1) the CVAE's $D_{KL}$ loss weight $\beta$, (2) the nominal latent dimension $d$, (3) the reconstruction-loss weighting $\gamma$, (4) the synthetic/real ratio used by TSR/TR, and (5) the strong-signal mask's max-intensity threshold \texttt{thr}. We consider the experimental configuration with $\beta=0.5$, $d=32$, $\gamma=1$, synthetic/real ratio = 1, \texttt{thr}=0.6 to be the reference configuration and the results of the other configurations are compared against it.

We report the bag-level accuracy for each training regimen (TR/TR: real only; TS/TR: synthetic only; TSR/TR: synthetic+real) under the naïve MIL approach (one majority-vote decision per bag, i.e. per biopsy slide), and the bootstrap difference $\Delta$ relative to the TR/TR baseline with its 95\% bootstrap confidence interval; (*) marks a 95\% CI excluding zero, and four additional bag-level metrics: balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC.

The bootstrap $\Delta$/CI/significance columns are computed for raw accuracy. We report the balanced accuracy as well; given the fairly balanced nature of this dataset for the binary IDH classification task, the two metrics are close.
The CbR row reports the bag-level accuracy and 95\% CI for it, and it does not represent a difference vs.\ TR/TR.

\subsection*{MIL tier results}\label{app:mil-results}

MIL tier experiments were performed for the most relevant configurations. Below are all the results with the same metrics as the downstream classifiers: accuracy, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC. We note that mi-SVM and Bayesian MIL are independent of $\beta$, and the others are built on top of the CVAE. For each experiment we report three tables: the bag-level classification metrics against the na\"{\i}ve MIL benchmark, the spatial coherence of the posterior maps, and the key-instance utility test.
% Full MIL tier results (methodology, spatial validation, shuffled-label controls) are reported in full in the project's own MIL findings reference. The five bag-level metrics below are reproduced here for the experiments in this family that have them, using the same convention as the downstream-classifier tables above: balanced accuracy and sensitivity/specificity as per-class recall (class 1 / class 0), AUC from the continuous bag-level diagnostic-class score. For mi-SVM/attention/CbR this score is the pooled, renormalized $(K{+}1)$-way posterior's diagnostic column; for Bayesian MIL's blind classification (\texttt{score\_bayes\_mil\_blind}) it is a softmax of the duplicate-bag-averaged posterior-predictive log-likelihood, since that path returns log-likelihoods, not a normalized probability. Bayesian MIL's Gibbs-sampled posterior is PCA-based and does not depend on $\beta$, which is why mi-SVM and Bayesian MIL are identical across \texttt{exp\_607}/\texttt{608}/\texttt{609} and again across \texttt{exp\_2.607}/\texttt{2.608}.

The spatial-coherence tables compare each tier's real fit with a refit under shuffled bag labels using the Moran's $I$ and the variogram spatial autocorrelation. We test the difference (real$-$shuffled) slide by slide. 

We compute a 95\% percentile CI from a bootstrap over the differences between real and shuffled metrics (10,000 resamples, seed 42); the real fit is considered more coherent than its control on a statistic only if the CI lies above zero, and a tier passes only if it does so on both statistics. 

In the key-instance utility test, a random forest (RF) is trained only on the training instances that a tier selects as key (non-diagnostic posterior mass below the median of the training fold), and classifies each held-out slide by majority vote over its selected instances. It is compared with the na\"{\i}ve MIL benchmark and with a forest trained on a size-matched random subset of the training instances.
